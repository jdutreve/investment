# Skill — pick the world events a macro investor needs

You read ONE day of Wikipedia's "Current events" portal: numbered items, each
with its category and the story it belongs to. You answer one question: **which
of these could move, within months, the macro picture of a long-horizon
investor holding US equities, US Treasuries, US corporate credit, gold and cash
— US rates, inflation, growth, credit spreads, energy prices, the dollar?**

**Most days, two or three items at most; many days, none.** The page is mostly
accidents, crime, sport, science, culture and local politics. Leaving them out
is the job, not a failure to notice.

**Kinds that qualify** (examples, not a list to fill):

- an election or a change of government that shifts the fiscal, trade or
  regulatory path of a large economy — above all the United States, then the
  euro area, China, Japan, the UK;
- a war, an escalation, a ceasefire or a blockade that touches energy
  producers, shipping lanes or a large economy;
- an energy or commodity supply shock: OPEC decisions, pipeline or refinery
  outages of scale, export bans, fuel rationing;
- fiscal and trade policy of a large economy: budgets, shutdowns, debt-ceiling
  standoffs, tariffs, sanctions, export controls;
- a central-bank decision OUTSIDE the Fed, the ECB and the SNB (these three are
  read elsewhere): the Bank of Japan, the Bank of England, the People's Bank of
  China, and others when the move is large or signals a global trend;
- a sovereign default, bailout or currency crisis; a large bank or market
  failure; a systemic cyber or infrastructure failure; a pandemic.

**Continuing stories.** You are given the titles already recorded in the last
weeks. Pick a continuing story only when this day CHANGES it — an escalation,
a ceasefire, a new front, a decision — not for another incident of the same kind.

**What to write for each pick.**

- `index` — the item's number.
- `title` — a headline in your words, specific enough to be found later
  ("Democrats win control of both chambers of the US Congress").
- `summary` — what the item says happened, in one to three sentences, and
  nothing it does not say.
- `why_it_matters` — the macro channel, in one or two sentences: which of
  rates, inflation, growth, credit, energy, the dollar, and in which direction
  it plausibly pushes. This is your reading, and it reads as one.
- `entities` — the countries, institutions and people named, as they would be
  searched for.

**The honesty rule.** The summary states only what the item's text supports.
What you add from your own knowledge goes in `why_it_matters` and is limited to
what you actually know. A fabricated sentence enters the corpus and is
retrieved months later as though it were sourced.
